RESEARCH PHASE DELIVERABLES

Tangible outputs produced during the research phase that feed directly into the financial model and report.

Desk Research
Earnings Model Seed
5-year historical financial data extracted from 10-Ks, normalized for one-time items. Associate-built in Excel.
Desk Research
Filing Summary Memo
2–5 page synthesis of key disclosures, risk factors, and MD&A themes from last 3 annual reports.
Management Access
Management Meeting Notes
Detailed notes from C-suite and segment meetings. Key takeaways, guidance parameters, strategic priorities.
Management Access
Key Assumption Sheet
Revenue growth, margin trajectory, capex, working capital — all grounded in management discussion and analyst judgment.
Expert Calls
Expert Call Notes (10–20)
Verbatim + synthesis. Key conviction builders or destroyers. Filed with compliance in most firms.
Channel Checks
Channel Data Analysis
Credit card / traffic / inventory data synthesized vs. consensus estimates. Leading indicator assessment.
Competitive Analysis
Peer Comp Universe
8–15 name comp group with standardized metrics: EV/EBITDA, P/E, EV/Sales, FCF yield, ROIC. Analyst-approved.
Industry
TAM Sizing Model
Bottom-up and top-down TAM estimate with sensitivity. Marketshare trajectory and capture assumptions.
All Sources
Source Citation Register
Compliance requirement at most firms: every factual claim in the initiation report must have a documented source.

§ 4

INITIATION REPORT ARCHITECTURE

A comprehensive initiation report typically runs 50–150 pages for a single name (shorter for sector overviews). Every section has a defined owner, required content, and compliance obligations. The table below and flow diagram below map each component in document order.

§ R.1
Cover Page
~1 page
Company name, ticker, exchange, date, report title ("Initiating Coverage"), rating (Buy/Hold/Sell), price target (12-month), current price, upside/downside %, analyst name, associate name, firm name, and required FINRA 2241 disclosure box summary. Must match all internal compliance-approved disclosures exactly.
Lead AnalystProductionCompliance (disclosures)
§ R.2
Executive Summary / Key Investment Points
1–3 pages
The most-read section. Typically 3–5 bulleted key points supporting the rating, concise thesis statement, 12-month price target and methodology, implied return including dividend, key risks to thesis (upside and downside), and near-term catalyst schedule. Written to stand alone — many PMs read only this section.
Lead Analyst
§ R.3
Investment Thesis — Bull / Base / Bear Cases
2–5 pages
Three-scenario narrative with explicit financial outcomes for each. Bull case: what has to go right (revenue upside, margin expansion, multiple re-rating). Base case: management targets, consensus-beating assumptions. Bear case: what can go wrong (revenue miss, margin compression, multiple compression). Probability weights optional but increasingly common. Variant view vs. consensus explicitly stated.
Lead Analyst
§ R.4
Company Overview & Business Model
5–15 pages
Corporate history and key milestones. Business model explanation: how the company makes money, pricing model, customer base, revenue mix (recurring vs. transactional). Segment descriptions with revenue and margin contribution. Geographic footprint. Key products/services. Management team bios (CEO, CFO, COO with tenure and track record). Board composition and governance assessment. Ownership structure (key shareholders, insider ownership %).
Lead AnalystAssociate
§ R.5
Industry & Sector Analysis
8–20 pages
Total addressable market (TAM) sizing with methodology disclosed. Market growth rate drivers (secular tailwinds, demographic, regulatory). Industry structure (fragmented vs. consolidated, oligopoly dynamics). Porter's Five Forces analysis. Regulatory environment and key pending rules. Technology disruption risks and opportunities. M&A landscape in the sector. Supply chain and input cost analysis.
Lead AnalystAssociate
§ R.6
Competitive Positioning
4–8 pages
Competitive moat assessment (cost leadership, differentiation, network effects, switching costs, regulatory barriers). Market share map with trend. Head-to-head comparison vs. top 3–5 competitors on key metrics: pricing, product, distribution, brand, R&D spend. Customer concentration analysis. Win/loss analysis where available. Assessment of management's competitive strategy and credibility.
Lead AnalystAssociate
§ R.7
Financial Analysis — Historical & Projected
10–25 pages
Revenue analysis and segment deep-dive. Gross margin and EBITDA margin bridge (historical and forecast). Working capital and cash conversion cycle. Capital expenditure cycle and return on invested capital (ROIC) trend. Free cash flow generation and uses (dividends, buybacks, M&A, deleveraging). Balance sheet health: leverage ratio, liquidity, covenant headroom. Consensus estimate comparison table (analyst vs. Street). Key metric sensitivities.
Lead AnalystAssociate (model / exhibits)
§ R.8
Valuation
5–12 pages
Primary valuation methodology with full disclosure of approach. DCF: explicit assumptions for WACC components, projection period, terminal growth rate, and enterprise-to-equity bridge. Trading comps: peer group with EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF yield on NTM+1 basis with relevant footnotes. Precedent transaction comps. SOTP (if applicable). Valuation summary chart ("football field" — range of outcomes by methodology). Sensitivity tables (2D: revenue growth vs. WACC; margin vs. multiple). 12-month price target derivation. Historical trading range analysis (where stock traded on key multiples over time).
Lead AnalystAssociate (exhibits)Research Director (PT sign-off)
§ R.9
Catalysts
2–4 pages
Near-term catalysts (3–12 months): earnings releases, investor days, FDA approvals, contract renewals, data readouts. Medium-term catalysts (1–3 years): product launches, market share gains, M&A, margin inflection. Each catalyst should have estimated timeline, probability, and financial impact. Bull case catalyst map vs. bear case catalyst map. Event-driven calendar.
Lead Analyst
§ R.10
Risk Factors
3–6 pages
Downside risks to rating and price target (ranked by severity and probability): execution risk, competitive risk, macro/rate risk, regulatory risk, customer concentration risk, key person risk, leverage / refinancing risk, litigation risk. Upside risks (if rated Hold or Sell): re-rating scenarios, faster-than-expected growth, margin upside, accretive M&A. ESG risk factors (increasingly required): governance risk, environmental liability, social / labor risk.
Lead AnalystCompliance (review for balance)
§ R.11
ESG Assessment (increasingly standard)
1–4 pages
Environmental: carbon footprint, energy intensity, climate transition risk, physical climate risk. Social: employee metrics (turnover, diversity), customer data privacy, supply chain labor practices, community impact. Governance: board independence, executive pay alignment, shareholder rights, related-party transaction history. MSCI ESG rating, Sustainalytics risk score, CDP score referenced.
Lead AnalystAssociate
§ R.12
Required Disclosures (FINRA Rule 2241 / Reg AC)
2–4 pages (boilerplate)
MANDATORY — compliance-generated, non-negotiable content: (1) Analyst certification (Reg AC §501): "I, [Analyst Name], certify that the views expressed herein accurately reflect my personal views..." (2) Rating distribution disclosure: firm-wide % of ratings in each category + % receiving IB services in each. (3) Company-specific IB disclosure: fees received in prior 12 months, investment banking relationship. (4) Analyst compensation disclosure (FINRA 2241(c)(3)). (5) Price target history chart (last 12 months). (6) Rating history chart (last 12 months). (7) Valuation methodology disclosure. (8) Risk disclosure. (9) Regulatory disclosures (MiFID II in EU; SEBI in India etc.). (10) Conflicts of interest disclosure. (11) Personal account trading restrictions disclosure.

§ 5

COMPLIANCE, REVIEW & PUBLICATION

The compliance and review phase is the most heavily regulated part of the process. FINRA Rule 2241 establishes strict requirements around supervisory review, IB firewall maintenance, analyst certification, and equal-access distribution. No report may be published without passing every checkpoint below.

§ 6.1

ONGOING COVERAGE DELIVERABLE TYPES

The full taxonomy of research products produced after initiation, each with its own production standard and compliance review requirement.

Earnings Season
Earnings Preview Note
1–4 pages. Estimate revisions, key metrics to watch, positioning ahead of print. Filed with compliance. Published 1–3 days before earnings.
Earnings Season
Earnings Flash / Day-Of Note
1–2 pages. Beat/miss vs. analyst and consensus. Published within 30–60 minutes of earnings release. Requires immediate compliance review — most firms have streamlined rapid-review process.
Earnings Season
Full Earnings Update
5–15 pages. Full model update, management call highlights, revised estimates, updated PT if changed. Published 1–2 days post-earnings. Full supervisory + compliance review required.
Catalyst-Driven
Rating Change Note
5–20 pages. Structured as mini-initiation. Explains thesis change. Requires full compliance cycle. Most significant deliverable post-initiation — high client impact.
Catalyst-Driven
Price Target Revision Note
1–5 pages. Documents PT change with new valuation basis, model changes, and revised estimates. Requires compliance approval and FINRA 2241(c)(4) disclosure update.
Catalyst-Driven
M&A / Deal Flash
1–3 pages. Initial reaction to announced deal. Impact on covered company. Accretion/dilution analysis. Published same day as announcement. Requires expedited compliance review.
Industry
Sector Overview / Thematic Note
10–50 pages. Quarterly or event-driven. Industry data synthesis, peer group comparison, macro overlay. Can be sector-wide or focused on a specific theme (AI impact, rate sensitivity, regulatory change).
Industry
Investor Day / Analyst Day Note
3–10 pages. Reaction to company-hosted event. New targets, financial framework, management credibility assessment. Published same day or next morning.
Client-Facing
Non-Deal Roadshow Support
Analyst prepares client presentation, coordinates with sales on meeting schedule, attends and presents. Analyst introduces management — no new information that isn't already public.
Model
Consensus Estimate Upload
After each model update, analyst uploads revised estimates to I/B/E/S (Refinitiv), Bloomberg, and FactSet estimate systems. Drives consensus change and screens for investors.
Compliance
Annual Disclosure Review
Compliance annually reviews all active coverage for updated IB relationship disclosures, rating distribution accuracy, and analyst certification renewals.
Discontinuation
Coverage Termination Note
"We are dropping coverage of [Company]. Our last rating was [X] with a [PT] price target." No new recommendation. Compliance-approved. Required to formally end coverage obligations.
